A clean mic and 3D game audio.
Free.

StreamLite removes background noise from your microphone with AI, turns games' surround sound into 3D audio for your headphones, and makes your headphones sound right. It works with Discord, OBS, Zoom, TeamSpeak and games, on Windows and Linux.

Download StreamLite All downloads Source code

Windows 10/11 and Linux (64-bit). Free and open source (GPL-3.0).

What it does

Microphone

AI noise removal

It removes keyboards, fans, PC hum, traffic, dogs, room echo and even a loud machine next to you. Voice isolation is built in: DeepFilterNet 3 (studio quality, runs on your graphics card on Windows).

Microphone

Sounds like a good mic

It includes a noise gate, auto level, compressor, de-esser, de-hum, EQ and voice styles. Record yourself and hear the result before your friends do.

Games

3D game audio

Games' 7.1 sound becomes 3D sound on any headphones, using measured human-like heads. You get true 360° placement, a footstep boost and a loud-sound tamer.

Games

A sound sphere you can turn

It shows where sounds come from all around you: the green ear-level ring, blue above, orange below. Drag it to look from any side. Open sphere takes out walls and floors, so footsteps are never muffled.

Games

Personalize 3D

Fly into the sphere and click where someone walks - in front, behind, left, right, above or below, with CS2's own footsteps. In about 4 minutes it finds which of 20 measured heads fits your ears. Practice mode trains you on your current settings for as long as you like.

Headphones

EQ for (almost) every headphone

Thousands of measured models from AutoEq, plus squig.link's reviewers for the rest (gaming headsets too) - StreamLite makes the EQ itself. Still missing? Report it in one click.

Headphones

Presets that switch - or play together

Each game gets its own sound, Music while Spotify plays, Chat while Discord is open. Presets together plays your game's preset on the game and Music's on Spotify at the same time.

Everything

Guided setup

The first start walks you through everything on your whole screen: mic, headphone EQ engine, your voice (hear it tuned before you go on), your headphones' EQ, 3D audio and your camera. Click a step along the bottom to go back to it; leave half way and it picks up where you left off.

Camera

AI camera

The "StreamLite Camera" virtual webcam can blur your room, act as a green screen without a green screen, and work like an AI gimbal camera with any webcam: tracking, gestures, presets, whiteboard and desk view.

Everything

Takes care of itself

It installs the free drivers it needs on Windows (and what it needs from your distro on Linux), updates itself, and has a Report a bug button.

Install

Pick your system. Every file is on the latest release, with its SHA-256 checksum in SHA256SUMS.txt.

  1. Download StreamLite-x.y.z-x64.msi and double-click it.
  2. If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info → Run anyway. StreamLite is free and isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen doesn't know it yet.
  3. Click through the installer and leave Start StreamLite now ticked. StreamLite gets a Start menu and desktop shortcut.

Running a portable (zip) copy from before 4.1.4? It moves to the installer when it updates - your settings stay.

Needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). The AI camera needs Windows 11. DeepFilterNet on the graphics card needs a DirectX 12 GPU; without one it runs on the CPU. With AI quality set to Low VRAM, all the AI together needs about 210 MB of video memory.

  1. Download StreamLite-x.y.z-linux.sh. It's one installer for every distro.
  2. Run it in a terminal: 
    bash StreamLite-*-linux.sh
    It asks for your password once, to install what StreamLite needs from your distro (Mono, ffmpeg, v4l-utils, v4l2loopback), installs StreamLite for you and adds it to your app menu. --system installs it for everyone (in /opt/streamlite).
  3. StreamLite starts with the guided setup - the same app, pages and features as on Windows.

Or in one line

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NermalYT/StreamLite/main/packaging/linux/get.sh | sh

Works on Ubuntu, Debian, Linux Mint, Pop!_OS, Zorin, Fedora, Nobara, Bazzite, openSUSE, Arch, Manjaro, EndeavourOS and CachyOS (64-bit). Remove it with Uninstall... in StreamLite's Settings, or bash StreamLite-*-linux.sh --uninstall. Coming from StreamLite 4.1.2 or older (the .deb, .rpm, AppImage, tarball or Arch package)? Run the installer once: it replaces the old StreamLite and keeps your settings, and StreamLite updates itself after that.

Linux needs: PipeWire (with pipewire-pulse) or PulseAudio, which every mainstream desktop already has. The installer adds everything else from your distro. StreamLite Camera needs the v4l2loopback module (the installer adds it where your distro has it; on Fedora it's in RPM Fusion). The AI runs on the CPU, or on an NVIDIA graphics card through CUDA: pick a graphics-card tier in Settings → AI quality and StreamLite downloads NVIDIA's libraries once (about 1.7 GB).

Set up in two minutes

  1. Pick your microphone. On Windows the guided setup opens by itself the first time and does the mic, your voice, the headphone EQ and 3D audio with you (about 6 minutes, picks up where you left off; updating with your settings saved, Keep my settings skips it). Otherwise choose your real mic on the Microphone page.
    Windows: the installer already set up VB-CABLE, the free virtual cable your clean mic travels through.
  2. Tell your apps. In Discord, go to Settings → Voice & Video and set Input Device to StreamLite Mic. Set Discord's Noise Suppression to None so your voice isn't cleaned twice. OBS (Audio Input Capture), Zoom, Teams, TeamSpeak, Mumble and games work the same way.
  3. Optional: headphones. On Headphones, click Find my headphones and pick your model.
    Windows: the guided setup switches Equalizer APO on for your headphones (Settings → Fallback EQ engine has it too). It applies the EQ to every app.
  4. Optional: 3D game audio. Turn on Game 3D audio and set your game's audio to 7.1 / surround. Click Test footsteps to hear it walk around you.
    Windows: the installer already set up Hi-Fi Cable, a separate virtual device for game sound, so the game never ends up in your mic.

Microphone

SettingWhat it does
Noise removalVoice isolation is built in - there's no engine to pick. StreamLite runs DeepFilterNet 3, on your graphics card on Windows or on the CPU (RNNoise on CPUs with fewer than 12 threads). Strength sets how far background noise is turned down.
Tune to my voice (Windows)
Five short recordings read like a teleprompter: big text, two lines in the middle, each word turning green the moment you say it (Windows' offline speech recognizer follows you word by word). Then StreamLite sets the best noise removal and quality your PC can run - DeepFilterNet on the graphics card when it fits, studio voice, the AI quality for your hardware - and shows what it chose.
Studio voiceOn by default (Microphone page). Makes any mic sound like a broadcast mic: while you talk it learns how your voice comes through this mic and takes out the mic's colouring (thin, boxy, harsh, muffled - at most -10 / +8 dB, smooth), rebuilds the treble cheap mics cut off (headsets, laptops, Bluetooth stop at 7-13 kHz) and catches plosive pops. Learned in about 5 seconds of talking and kept per mic; Learn again after moving the mic. Adds no delay.
Record a sound to remove (Windows)For a sound noise removal lets through: a TV, kids, music. Let it play, stay quiet and press Record (Tuning & EQ → Noise removal). In 8 seconds StreamLite learns what the noise remover leaves of it and takes that out from then on (adds 21 ms). Your own voice is learned in the background while you talk, so it isn't mistaken for the sound. For steady noise like a fan it tells you the noise remover already handles it.
AI quality (Windows, Settings)Where voice isolation and the AI camera run and how big their models are. Low VRAM: small 16-bit models, about 210 MB of video memory, the camera in 1080p. Normal: the full models, about 430-490 MB. High VRAM: the camera's cut-out worked out at full HD - cleaner hair and edges, fewer halos - about 860-890 MB. Full CPU: nothing on the graphics card (slowest). StreamLite picks what fits your graphics card the first time.
Let other apps use the raw mic tooOn by default (Microphone page). Discord, OBS or a game can still pick your real microphone directly while StreamLite runs, and StreamLite gets it in Windows' 10 ms packets: about 22 ms from mic to cable. Off: StreamLite holds the mic alone in its smallest packets (about 7 ms) and other apps get silence from the raw mic. Noise removal adds its own delay: RNNoise 20 ms, DeepFilterNet 50-60 ms.
StrengthHow far background noise is turned down. 70% sounds natural. Go up for loud rooms.
Noise gateMutes what's left of the background while you don't talk. Smooth fades out; Hard cuts.
Auto levelLifts a quiet voice and tames a loud one, so friends hear you at the same level. Turn off Discord's automatic gain control if you use it.
Compressor, de-esser, de-hum, low cutEven out your voice, soften "s" sounds, remove 50/60 Hz mains hum and remove rumble.
Voice stylesReady-made sounds to try: Natural, Clear for chat, Broadcast, Noisy room, Late night, Deep & warm, Raw. Your own tuning is kept under My settings.
Test your voiceRecords a few sentences and plays them back with your settings, so you hear what others hear.
Mic inputFor audio interfaces and multi-channel mics. Auto uses only the inputs that carry your voice, so an XLR mic on input 1 isn't made 6 dB quieter by an empty input 2, and an out-of-phase laptop channel can't cancel you. You can also mix all inputs or pick one. On Windows it's under Tuning & EQ → Noise removal.

Headphones

Headphone correction

Find my headphones searches AutoEq, which has thousands of headphones and earbuds measured on calibrated rigs. It downloads the correction for yours, and every profile sounds the way it was designed on top of that. Each headset or pair of earbuds keeps its own correction: switch devices and StreamLite switches corrections.

Models AutoEq hasn't measured come from squig.link: about 150 reviewers' measurement databases, gaming headsets included (HyperX Cloud III, Razer BlackShark V3, Kraken V4, Corsair HS80, Logitech G435...). They publish raw measurements, so StreamLite makes the EQ itself against the Harman target. Still not there? Missing? Report it opens a ready-made report on GitHub with your model - you send it yourself.

Profiles

With Auto-detect on, StreamLite picks the sound for what you're doing:

  • Your game: a profile per game, tuned for footsteps and clarity.
  • Chat: while Discord, Zoom, Teams, TeamSpeak, Mumble, Skype, Slack, Signal, Telegram, WhatsApp or Webex is open. Voices are clear and easy on the ears for long calls.
  • Music: while Spotify plays.
  • Movies & videos: everything else.

Games win over chat, so you get game sound while you talk on Discord in a match. Pick a profile by hand to turn auto-detect off.

Presets together (Windows, with 3D audio on): every preset plays at once on its own sound - your game's preset on the game (it plays through StreamLite) and Music's on Spotify or Chat's on Discord (straight to your headphones). The game hears exactly its own preset; your headphone correction stays under both.

StreamLite plays every app's sound itself and puts your headphone EQ on it. On Windows, when it can't hold your headphones, the EQ runs in Equalizer APO instead. On Linux everything plays through StreamLite - Out (the default output while StreamLite runs); apps kept out of 3D, and apps set to your headphones directly, still get your EQ.

3D game audio

  1. Turn on Game 3D audio.
  2. Set your game's audio output to 7.1 / surround / home theater. Without that, there's nothing to place around you.
  3. Games with their own 3D audio (HRTF), like CS2 and Valorant, skip StreamLite 3D and go straight to your headphones. You can set this per game under Headphones → Game profiles.
  4. Swapping headsets is fine: plug one in, or pick another output in Windows' sound flyout, and 3D audio moves there by itself.

The sound sphere

You're the white dot in the middle, nose to the front: ABOVE in blue, the EAR LEVEL ring in green, BELOW in orange. Game sound lights up the ear-level ring where it comes from; sudden sounds (footsteps, shots) flash at their direction.

Right-drag the sphere to turn it and look from any side. Right double-click puts it back. The tests (footsteps, 360° walk, above / below) show where each step should come from.

Settings worth knowing

  • Personalize 3D: the window fills your monitor and flies you into the sphere, drawn by your graphics card at your monitor's refresh rate. Someone walks in one spot in front of you, behind, left, right, above or below - Counter-Strike 2's own recorded footsteps when CS2 is installed (read from the game, never shipped). Every footstep starts with you facing forward; snap the view to where you hear them (mouse, arrow keys or WASD) and click, or right-click when you can't tell and it shows you. First it finds your head among all 20 (blind, narrowed down as it goes), then it tunes your head size, cue sharpening and floor contrast. About 4 minutes; saved for those headphones after every part. Then practise as long as you like.
  • Practice (Windows, next to Tune 3D to my ears): the same footsteps from the six directions with your current settings - nothing is tuned - for as long as you like, with your score. Esc ends it.
  • 16D: Off, Music apps (Spotify and music players circle your head) or All apps (everything but games and voice chat).
  • Direct to headphones is built in: StreamLite is the audio engine, so game and app sound plays through it straight to your headphones on the lowest-delay path. Discord's full-screen share still gets every app's sound (Discord takes it from each app itself).
  • Open sphere: no walls, floors or crates between you and a sound - footsteps above you come straight from above, never muffled.
  • Head model: 20 measured heads, each at its full measurement - KU100 (natural), KEMAR (sharper cues) and 18 real people. Ears differ: the head closest to yours places sounds best. Personalize 3D finds it.
  • Footstep boost and Loud sound tamer keep footsteps audible after gunfire.
  • True 360° places each sound at its exact angle instead of between 7 virtual speakers.
  • Keep these apps out of 3D (Windows): Discord, Spotify and browsers stay normal stereo.

Games' 7.1 has no height channels. Above and below come from cue sharpening and the test signals, so how much you hear depends on the game.

AI camera (Windows 11)

On the Camera page, turn on StreamLite Camera (AI), then pick StreamLite Camera as your webcam in Discord, OBS, Zoom or Teams. You can:

  • blur or dim your room
  • replace it with a solid green screen, a picture or a looping video
  • let Auto light (with Face exposure and HDR) even out a dark room or a bright window

AI tracking & framing

StreamLite Camera works like an AI gimbal camera (Insta360 Link 2 style) with any webcam: it moves, zooms and turns a window inside your camera's picture. With a 4K camera, up to 2x costs no sharpness.

  • AI tracking of one person (click a face on the preview to switch) or a group (everyone stays in)
  • Framing: head and shoulders, half body, whole body or your own zoom, with headroom
  • Gestures beside your face, each with its own switch (Camera → Gestures & air drawing): open palm = tracking on/off, V = whiteboard or air drawing, L = zoom (move your hand up or down). Gestures never turn AI tracking on by themselves - only the palm does, when its switch is on.
  • Air drawing: set V does to air drawing, make a V, then point your index finger and write in the air ("Hi!"); any other hand pose lifts the pen, a V stops. Pen colour, thickness and fading are yours; Clear drawing wipes it.
  • Animations apps see: flip upside down, spin around, barrel roll, zoom punch and shake - buttons on the Camera page and hotkeys
  • Tracking zone (it never shows anything outside it) and up to six pause zones (step in and it frames the zone and holds still)
  • Your own view: drag the preview to move, mouse wheel to zoom, six presets (also on hotkeys)
  • Whiteboard (found automatically, squared off, writing enhanced) and desk view (an area you mark)
  • Level a tilted camera, upside down for hanging or overhead cameras, a vertical 9:16 guide for TikTok and Shorts
  • Privacy: apps see "Camera paused" (button, hotkey, or automatically when you leave)

The camera page also has every setting of your real webcam: exposure, focus, white balance and format.

How good the cut-out is follows AI quality in Settings: High VRAM gives the cleanest hair and edges; on a graphics card with little video memory, pick Low VRAM.

Hotkeys

On the Hotkeys page, Add hotkey opens a popup: pick the area (Microphone, Headphones & 3D, Camera, StreamLite), the action, and press the key - any key with Ctrl / Alt / Shift / Win, or a mouse side button. Click a hotkey to change it, × to remove it.

  • Microphone: mute, push to talk / to mute, noise removal, studio voice, voice lock, across the room, record a sound to remove, raw mic, voice styles
  • Headphones & 3D: 3D on/off, presets, 16D (next / off / music apps / all apps), open sphere, presets together, 3D for stereo, limiter, volume up / down, head model, headphone correction
  • Camera: backgrounds, AI tracking, framing, upside down, HDR, zones, privacy, each gesture, air drawing and clearing it, and the animations (flip upside down, spin, barrel roll, zoom punch, shake)

Updates and bug reports

Update StreamLite when it starts is on by default (Settings → Updates and bug reports). When StreamLite starts, it does this:

  1. Asks GitHub for the newest release.
  2. Downloads the file that matches how you installed it: the MSI, the zip or the Linux installer.
  3. Checks the download against the release's SHA-256 sums.
  4. Installs it and restarts. Your mic drops out for a few seconds.

The MSI asks Windows for permission once, unless StreamLite runs as administrator. On Linux nothing is asked: StreamLite updates itself in your home folder (an install for everyone asks for your password). Use Check now any time.

Report a bug is in the sidebar, in Settings and in the tray menu. Pick what kind of problem it is, say what happened and how to make it happen, add pictures - your StreamLite version, system and details are added for you. Send opens the bug form on GitHub with all of it filled in (a free GitHub account); add your pictures there.

Help

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

That's SmartScreen. It warns about any app that isn't code-signed or downloaded often yet. Click More info → Run anyway. StreamLite is open source: you can read every line or build it yourself.

StreamLite says Windows blocks the microphone

Windows' privacy settings keep desktop apps away from your mic, so every app (StreamLite included) only gets silence. Click Open privacy settings on the Microphone page, or go to Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone. Turn on Microphone access and Let desktop apps access your microphone. StreamLite hears you within a few seconds.

I use an audio interface (Focusrite, Behringer, MOTU...)

Pick the interface as your microphone. Mic input: Auto finds the input your mic is plugged into after a second of talking, so an empty second input doesn't make you quieter. If you use two mics on purpose, set Mic input to Mix all inputs, or pick the input by hand.

My gaming headset has its own 7.1 / virtual surround

If you use StreamLite 3D, turn the headset's own surround off in its app (G HUB, NGENUITY, Synapse, iCUE, SteelSeries GG). Otherwise games get made 3D twice and sound washed out. StreamLite shows a hint on the 3D Audio page when it detects one.

My game gets quiet whenever someone talks on Discord

That's Windows, not StreamLite. By default it turns every other sound down 80% while a call app has a voice stream open. In StreamLite's Settings → Audio devices, turn on Keep game volume during calls. Discord has a setting of its own too: Voice & Video → Attenuation, which should be 0%.

StreamLite says my microphone is clipping

Your mic is so loud that it distorts before StreamLite gets the sound, and no effect can undo that. Turn the gain down on the mic or audio interface (the knob), or lower its level in Windows Sound settings → Recording (on Linux, in your sound settings). StreamLite's Auto level brings you back up cleanly.

Copying details for a bug report

Settings → Updates and bug reports → Copy details puts your StreamLite and system version, what StreamLite is doing and your audio devices on the clipboard. Your user name is taken out. Paste it into the bug report so device problems can be fixed. On Linux, run streamlite --details.

My friends can't hear me

Check these:

  • In Discord (or the app you use), the input device is StreamLite Mic.
  • StreamLite is running. Look for the tray icon.
  • Virtual mic is on.
  • Your real mic is selected and its meter moves when you talk.

Set the app's input device to StreamLite - In (Discord may call it StreamLite Mic), not "Default". Health → Run setup check checks the whole path.

My voice sounds robotic or choppy
  • Lower Strength.
  • Make sure the app's own noise suppression is None. Krisp on top of StreamLite causes artefacts.
  • On a weak PC, set AI quality (Settings) to Low VRAM, or Full CPU when there's no graphics card.
Friends hear my fan, or my voice gets quieter the longer I talk
  • Turn off the call app's own processing - StreamLite already does it, and two of them fight: in Discord, Settings → Voice & Video: Noise suppression: None, Echo cancellation off, Automatic gain control off. An app's automatic gain control is what turns a voice down the longer you talk.
  • A USB mic with a pattern switch (Blue Yeti and similar): set it to cardioid and talk into its front from about a hand's width away. In stereo or omni it hears the whole room, fan included.
  • Keep noise removal on and Strength at 70% or more; "Noisy room" makes the pauses near-silent.
Friends hear my game or music

Something plays into the mic cable. Never pick CABLE Input or StreamLite Mic as a speaker in a game or app. StreamLite keeps game sound on its own device (Hi-Fi Cable on Windows) for exactly this reason.

I don't hear any 3D effect

Check these:

  • The game's audio is set to 7.1 / surround.
  • The game plays to StreamLite Game Audio. That's the default output while 3D is on.
  • Test footsteps walks around you.

Some games (CS2, Valorant, ...) use their own HRTF; StreamLite sends those straight to your headphones on purpose.

Linux: there's no tray icon (GNOME)

GNOME hides tray icons. Install the AppIndicator and KStatusNotifierItem Support extension. Without it, StreamLite keeps running: start it again from the app menu to open its window.

Linux: "pactl not found" or no devices

Run the installer again (it adds pactl), or install pulseaudio-utils (Debian, Ubuntu, Fedora, openSUSE) or libpulse (Arch). On PipeWire you also need pipewire-pulse, which desktops have by default. Then Health → Run setup check.

Linux: StreamLite Camera isn't offered

It needs the v4l2loopback module and ffmpeg. Run the installer again; on Fedora enable RPM Fusion first. Then turn StreamLite Camera on: it asks for your password once to add the camera.

Linux: Flatpak apps (Discord, OBS)

They see StreamLite Mic like any other microphone through PipeWire/PulseAudio. Nothing extra to set up.

Uninstall

To remove StreamLite:

  • Windows: Settings → Apps → StreamLite, or Uninstall... in StreamLite's Settings. This puts your sound devices back the way Windows had them. VB-CABLE, Hi-Fi Cable and Equalizer APO stay installed; remove them in Settings → Apps if you like.
  • Linux: Uninstall... in StreamLite's Settings, or bash StreamLite-*-linux.sh --uninstall. StreamLite's sound devices go with it and your default devices come back. (Installed an older StreamLite from a .deb, .rpm, PKGBUILD or AppImage? Remove that with your package manager, or delete the AppImage.)
What does StreamLite send over the internet?

Nothing about you. It only connects to:

  • GitHub, to check for updates (you can turn this off)
  • AutoEq on GitHub, when you search for your headphones
  • vb-audio.com and SourceForge, when you click a driver install button

Bug reports are only sent when you file them yourself on GitHub.

Still stuck? Search the known issues or report a bug.
